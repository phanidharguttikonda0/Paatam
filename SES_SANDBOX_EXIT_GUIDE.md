# AWS SES Setup, Deliverability & Sandbox Exit Guide

This guide covers everything needed to configure **Amazon Simple Email Service (SES)** for the Paatam application:
1. **Why emails were landing in Spam** and how to fix deliverability with DKIM, SPF, and DMARC.
2. **Why only `@paatam.in` addresses are currently accepted** (SES Sandbox restriction).
3. **Immediate workaround** to test external emails (like personal Gmail addresses) in development right now.
4. **Step-by-step instructions** to request AWS Production Access to send to all email providers globally.

---

## 1. Why Did Emails Land in the Spam Folder?

When email providers (Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo) receive an email from `noreply@paatam.in`, their automated spam filters check three cryptographic standards:
1. **DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail)**: Proves that the email was actually signed and sent by the owner of `paatam.in`.
   - **Current Status**: `DkimVerificationStatus: Pending` in AWS SES. Because the infrastructure was recreated, new DKIM keys were generated. Until the 3 CNAME records are added to your DNS provider, emails are sent without a valid DKIM signature, which Gmail flags as suspicious/spam.
2. **SPF (Sender Policy Framework)**: A DNS record telling mail servers that Amazon SES servers have permission to send emails for `paatam.in`.
3. **DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication)**: Confirms that SPF and DKIM are aligned and tells Gmail what to do if an email claims to be from `paatam.in`.

Once you add the DNS records below, Gmail and Outlook will verify the sender and route emails directly into the **Primary Inbox**.

## 2. Required DNS Records for `paatam.in`

Log into your Domain Registrar / DNS provider (e.g., **GoDaddy, Hostinger, Cloudflare, Namecheap, Route 53**) and add the following records:

### A. The 3 DKIM CNAME Records (Generated by Terraform)

> **Note for Hostinger/GoDaddy/Cloudflare users**: Most DNS providers automatically append your domain (`paatam.in`). If your provider does this, only enter the text before `._domainkey` in the Name field, or enter the full name if required.

| Type | Name / Host | Target / Points To | TTL |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CNAME** | `yvkvhja4eudqdrlsn2gfe7xqxed3fjos._domainkey` | `yvkvhja4eudqdrlsn2gfe7xqxed3fjos.dkim.amazonses.com` | Auto / 3600 |
| **CNAME** | `3lk6dpbvkoeih4zs7dxbi4g3uasmnttd._domainkey` | `3lk6dpbvkoeih4zs7dxbi4g3uasmnttd.dkim.amazonses.com` | Auto / 3600 |
| **CNAME** | `jiuf6oighq24cy2v2z2qz7n4bur3azga._domainkey` | `jiuf6oighq24cy2v2z2qz7n4bur3azga.dkim.amazonses.com` | Auto / 3600 |

### B. SPF Record (Authorize Amazon SES to Send Emails)
| Type | Name / Host | Value | TTL |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **TXT** | `@` (or leave blank / `paatam.in`) | `v=spf1 include:amazonses.com ~all` | Auto / 3600 |

*If you already have an SPF record for Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 (e.g. `v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all`), simply add `include:amazonses.com` before `~all`:*
```text
v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:amazonses.com ~all
```

### C. DMARC Policy (Protects Reputation & Guarantees Inbox Delivery)
| Type | Name / Host | Value | TTL |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **TXT** | `_dmarc` (or `_dmarc.paatam.in`) | `v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:postmaster@paatam.in` | Auto / 3600 |

## 3. Why Was Only `@paatam.in` Accepted? (SES Sandbox Mode)

By default, every new AWS account is placed in the **Amazon SES Sandbox** in region `ap-south-2` (Hyderabad).

### Sandbox Restrictions:
- **Strict Recipient Filtering**: You can **ONLY** send emails to:
  - Any address on verified domains (which is why `anything@paatam.in` works).
  - Explicitly verified email addresses.
  - Attempting to send to any unverified address (like `someone@gmail.com`) is rejected by AWS with:
    ```text
    MessageRejected: Email address is not verified. The following identities failed the check in region AP-SOUTH-2: someone@gmail.com
    ```
- **Sending Limits**: Maximum 200 emails per 24 hours, and 1 email per second.

## 4. Quick Workaround for Dev Testing (Takes 1 Minute)

If you want to test OTP emails with your personal email (e.g., `yourname@gmail.com`) **immediately** without waiting for AWS approval:

1. Open the [AWS SES Console](https://console.aws.amazon.com/ses/).
2. In the top navigation bar, ensure the region is **Asia Pacific (Hyderabad) `ap-south-2`**.
3. In the left menu, click **Identities** (under *Configuration*).
4. Click the orange **Create identity** button.
5. Select **Email address**.
6. Type your personal email address (e.g., `yourname@gmail.com`) and click **Create identity**.
7. AWS will immediately send a verification link to your Gmail inbox.
8. Open the email and click the confirmation link.
9. **Done!** You can now request OTPs in dev to that email address without any errors.

## 5. How to Move Out of the Sandbox (Request Production Access)

To allow Paatam to send OTPs and invitation emails to **any** student, teacher, or admin email address globally, request production access:

### Step 1: Open Account Dashboard
1. Log in to the [AWS Management Console](https://console.aws.amazon.com/ses/).
2. Confirm region is **Asia Pacific (Hyderabad) `ap-south-2`**.
3. In the left menu, click **Account dashboard**.

### Step 2: Click Request Production Access
In the banner stating **"Account status: Sandbox"**, click **Request production access** (or **Edit account details**).

### Step 3: Complete the Application Form
Fill in the following details (copy and paste the prepared use-case):

- **Mail Type**: `Transactional`
- **Website URL**: `https://paatam.in`
- **Use Case Description**:
  ```text
  Paatam (https://paatam.in) is an educational school management and student learning platform.
  Emails sent via Amazon SES are strictly transactional, including:
  1. Time-sensitive 6-digit numeric OTP verification codes for user registration, multi-factor authentication, and password resets.
  2. School onboarding invitations for corporate admins, branch admins, and teachers.
  3. Student assignment notifications and academic announcements.
  
  All recipients are registered platform users who explicitly initiate requests via our web application. We do not send marketing, promotional, or bulk unrequested emails. Bounces and complaints are monitored through Amazon SNS topics and automatically suppressed.
  
  Expected volume: ~1,000 to 5,000 transactional emails per day.
  ```
- **Recipient Opt-in**: Check the box confirming that recipients explicitly trigger or opt-in to notifications.
- **Region**: `ap-south-2` (Hyderabad).

### Step 4: Submit
AWS Support typically reviews and approves transactional requests within **12 to 24 hours**. Once approved, your daily quota increases to 50,000+ emails/day, and you can email any domain in the world.

## 6. Verification Commands

You can verify the status of your domain and DKIM keys at any time from your terminal:

```bash
# 1. Check Domain Verification Status
aws ses get-identity-verification-attributes \
  --identities paatam.in \
  --region ap-south-2

# 2. Check DKIM Verification Status (Turns from "Pending" to "Success" after DNS propagates)
aws ses get-identity-dkim-attributes \
  --identities paatam.in \
  --region ap-south-2

# 3. Check Current Send Quota (Shows 200 in Sandbox, 50,000+ in Production)
aws ses get-send-quota \
  --region ap-south-2
```
